Upperform inbox assistant · how the sorting works

How it decides

Every email that arrives gets exactly one of four verdicts. Two things decide it — a model that reads, and a set of rules that can overrule the model. This is both, and what happens after.

The question it asks first

Before any of the four labels, before anything else, there is one distinction that decides most of the inbox.

Is this about Upperform’s utility consulting work, or about Yancey’s own personal and business administration?

Consulting work is real even when the message is one casual line. Personal admin — tax prep, retirement plans, payroll vendors, insurance, banking, contractor invoices — is noise even when it is a named human being, warmly introduced, proposing a specific meeting time. Even when Yancey has already replied in that thread himself.

Politeness, a real name and a real calendar ask are not evidence of relevance. That sentence exists because of one email in Yancey’s own training set, and it is the hardest case in the whole system.

The four verdicts

Exactly one per email. The colour of each card is the colour of the Outlook category it produces, so this page and his inbox use the same language.

opportunity

Live engagement work needing his awareness or response. Deliverables sent for review, status updates, client replies on decks and plans, direction from Chuck, peer-review threads, business-development follow-ups.

Category AI-Action · a draft is written
admin

Scheduling logistics, calendar invites, Teams meeting notices, routine confirmations. Things that need at most a one-line reply.

Category AI-Action · a draft is written
noise

No reply needed. Vendor and channel sales outreach, payment and invoice notifications, courtesy acknowledgements that close a thread, newsletters and automated alerts.

Category AI-Processed only · nothing written
flag_for_yancey

Surface it, never draft. Legal, contractual, HR, press, disputes — anything where a wrong word costs money. And anything asking a price he has not already quoted.

Category AI-Review · deliberately no draft

Two of the four produce a draft, and two do not.

opportunity and admin are the draftable pair. noise and flag_for_yancey never produce one — but for opposite reasons. Noise is ignored because nothing is owed. A flag is held back precisely because something is owed, and it is the kind of thing an assistant should not attempt.

Who actually decides

The model proposes. The rules dispose. That order matters, and it is the whole safety design.

The classifier (gpt-5.6-luna) reads the newest message in the thread and returns four things: a label, a confidence from 0 to 1, whether a reply is genuinely warranted, and a one-sentence reason. It never sees the quoted history — only the newest message, because that is the part being answered.

Then a chain of deterministic rules runs over the result. Several of them can overrule the model outright, and they read the whole thread, quoted history included, up to 12,000 characters. None of that extra text is ever sent to the model. It exists only so the rules can see what the model was not shown.

This is why the model getting it wrong is survivable.

A model can be talked into things. A regular expression cannot. The rules below are the part of the system that does not have a bad day — and every one of them can only make the outcome more cautious, never less.

The rules, in the order they run

Each one can add a flag. Flags are recorded against the email so any decision can be explained after the fact.

1

Could the answer be read at all?

If the model returns something unparseable, the label becomes flag_for_yancey rather than a guess. Flag: unparseable_label.

2

Is anyone asking a price?

Scans the whole thread. If somebody is asking what something costs and no fee has already been quoted, the label is forced to flag_for_yancey. Flag: pricing_guard.

3

Is this legal, contractual or personnel?

Lawsuits, subpoenas, attorneys, breach of contract, indemnities, severance, harassment, press. Any of them forces flag_for_yancey. Flag: sensitivity_guard.

4

Was the model sure enough?

Below 0.6 confidence the email is surfaced rather than drafted. The label stands; the draft does not happen. Flag: below_confidence_min.

5

Is this even the right mailbox?

A draft is only written if the mailbox it was authorised for is on the message’s To or Cc. Re-point the mail account at somebody else and drafting stops rather than following it. Flag: mailbox_not_addressed.

6

Then, and only then, a draft

Written by a second model, checked against the source for invented figures, given his sign-off, and left in the Drafts folder. It cannot be sent. That is a separate guarantee, covered in the other note.

The money rule, in detail

It is the one that costs real money to get wrong, so it is the one built most carefully — and the one that has been wrong twice.

A naïve version would flag any email containing the word “cost”. That is useless in a consulting inbox where half the mail discusses budgets. So the rule has two halves, and both require two things to sit near each other rather than merely appear somewhere.

To fire, a money word (fee, cost, estimate, scope of work…) must sit within 60 characters of a question form (can you provide, how much…). About one clause.

To stand down, a real figure must sit within 200 characters of fee-stating language — “$175K to $200K on a fixed-fee basis”. A capital-plan number with no such words near it does not count as a quote.

Both windows were measured against real mail, not guessed:

MUST FIRE   one sentence     19 chars apart   can you provide ↔ estimate
MUST FIRE   buried in a chain  19               can you provide ↔ estimate
MUST NOT    status request   107              send me your    ↔ cost
MUST NOT    share the deck   122              can you share   ↔ budget

Both times it was wrong, Yancey’s own mail found it.

First it read too little — only the newest message — and missed a fee request sitting two replies down a quoted chain. Then, reading whole threads, it read too much: “please send me your updates” in one message and the word “cost” in another, a paragraph apart, looked together like somebody asking a price. The proximity windows are what closed the gap between those two failures.

What he actually sees

All of the above surfaces as three colours on his existing inbox. There is nothing new to log into.

In OutlookMeansWhat to do
AI-Processed Seen, logged, judged not to need him. Nothing. It stays out of the morning summary.
AI-Action Needs him, and a reply has been written. Read the draft, change what he wants, press Send — or bin it.
AI-Review Needs him, and the assistant deliberately wrote nothing. Handle it himself. The reason is recorded against the email.
AI-Drafted A reply is sitting in the Drafts folder right now. Disappears from the summary on its own once he sends it.

Every verdict is explainable after the fact.

Each email gets a row recording the label, the confidence, the one-sentence reason, what the sender wanted, and which rules fired. If a decision ever looks wrong, the answer to “why did it do that?” is a lookup, not a guess.
