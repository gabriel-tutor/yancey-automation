The inbox assistant, and what it can’t do

It watches your mailbox, sorts what arrives, and writes replies for you to review. It has no ability to send them. This is how that works.

What happens to one email

Everything runs inside the Outlook you already use. Nothing new to log into, nothing new to check.

What you’ll actually see

Coloured flags on the emails you already have open. They appear on their own, within a couple of minutes of the mail landing.

Junk

Read and dismissed

Vendor pitches, invoice notifications, “Received, thank you!”. Flagged so you know it was seen, then left alone.

Needs you

Worth your eyes

Live engagement work — a deck in review, a status update, an action Chuck has put your way.

Sensitive

Surfaced, never drafted

Legal, contracts, HR, press, disputes — and anything asking your price. The assistant will not put words in your mouth on these.

Drafted

A reply is waiting

A draft sits in your Drafts folder, threaded to the original, recipients filled in. Read it, change it, send it or bin it.

Why it cannot send mail

Not a setting we chose and could change by accident. The permission was never requested, so the path does not exist.

We tested this by trying to break it.

The assistant was pointed at a test mailbox and deliberately instructed to send an email. Microsoft refused it. That test now runs before the assistant is connected to any mailbox, and it has to fail — that’s how we know the guarantee is real rather than assumed.

Your morning summary

One email, 7:30 Central, weekdays. Monday looks back across the weekend so nothing from Friday afternoon slips.

Needs you

What arrived that wants your judgement, most urgent first, with one line on why it was flagged.

Drafts waiting

Replies sitting in your Drafts folder, with a count of how many business days each has been waiting.

Follow-ups due

Threads that have gone quiet for three business days, so a live conversation doesn’t die of silence.

Once you send a draft it disappears from the list on its own. There’s nothing to tick off — pressing Send is how the assistant knows you’ve dealt with it.

How we’ll switch it on

Deliberately slow at the start. You get to correct its judgement before it writes a word on your behalf.

15 min
We connect it, together. You sign in on Microsoft’s own screen, on your own computer. I never see your password. You’ll read the permission list before you approve it — that’s the moment to check sending isn’t on it.
Days 1–3
Flags only. No drafts at all. Coloured flags start appearing on your real mail. You tell me which ones it got wrong, and I tune its judgement — usually the same day.
Week 2
Drafts switch on. Replies begin appearing in your Drafts folder and the morning summary starts. Still nothing sends itself.
After
Handover. A week running clean, then everything transfers to you — accounts, access, documentation. You can revoke my access, and the assistant’s, from your own settings at any time.

Five things I need from you

All small, and all things only you can answer.

  • Your email signature Copy and paste how you normally sign off to someone you’ve not met before — title, phone, anything else you include.
  • A booking link Calendly or similar, if you use one, so drafts can offer it when proposing a call. If you don’t, no problem.
  • The summary time I’ve set 7:30 AM Central, Monday to Friday. Say the word if you’d rather have it later.
  • Follow-up patience How many days of silence before a thread shows up as needing a nudge? I’ve assumed three business days.
  • Anyone to always flag Specific people or subjects you want surfaced with no draft written, beyond legal, HR, press and pricing, which are already covered.
