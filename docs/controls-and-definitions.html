6 AI-Action, AI-Drafted, and failed draftsClarified & fixed

Asking this found a real defect, so the answer comes in two parts.

What the four labels mean

CategoryMeaning
AI-ProcessedThe assistant read it. Applied to everything it touches.
AI-ActionIt needs you, and the assistant is handling it.
AI-ReviewThe assistant deliberately did not draft. Over to you.
AI-DraftedA reply is waiting in your Drafts folder.

AI-Action and AI-Drafted travel together: the first says the message matters, the second says a draft exists for it. AI-Action and AI-Review are opposites and never appear on the same message.

What was wrong

They could appear together, until you asked. A message classed as needing action got AI-Action; if the draft then failed to generate, the assistant added AI-Review without removing AI-Action, so it carried both and told you two contradictory things. That is fixed — AI-Action is now cleared whenever no draft is written.

What happens when a draft fails

  • The empty draft is deleted, so nothing half-written is left in your Drafts folder.
  • The message is tagged AI-Review and appears in your morning summary.
  • The failure is logged and an alert is raised to me, not to you.

A failed draft therefore looks exactly like a deliberate hold from your side. You never have to know the difference, and nothing is lost.

Are attachments read or processed?

Yes, as of 2 September 2026 — this changed, and it is worth reading. Until now the assistant recorded only that attachments existed. It now opens most of them, because triaging a message whose substance sits in the attachment rather than the body was guesswork otherwise.

What this means for your documents. Word, PowerPoint and Excel files are opened on the automation server and never leave it. PDFs and images are different: those are sent to the model, because a scanned page has no text to pull out of it. So a PDF a client sends you does travel to OpenAI, under the same no-training terms as the message bodies already do. If that is not acceptable for a particular client, say so and it goes back off.

Drafts may now refer to what an attachment actually says, rather than only acknowledging that one arrived. They still cannot invent a figure that is not in the document, and they still cannot quote one that was not read.

The rest of the detail. Sending PDFs and images to the model is also what makes a photographed document or a screenshot readable at all. Plain text, CSV and similar are decoded directly on the server. Anything it cannot open — a Power BI file, an old .xls, an archive, a video — is named in the summary as not read, rather than passed over in silence. Only the first few attachments on a message are opened, and only the first three of those that need the model — everything past that is named rather than read. Nothing above 25 MB is touched. Signature logos are recognised and skipped without being opened, so the assistant does not spend a reading on being told what a company logo is.

Turning it off is a one-line configuration change and a redeploy, and the assistant goes straight back to recording only that an attachment was there. It is a single switch, not a rewrite, so changing your mind about this costs nothing.

Where are the decision logs, and who can reach them?

On the automation server, in a table called agent_email_log_yancey. Gabriel can see it. Nobody else has an account.

Each row holds the sender, subject, the label, the assistant's one-line reasoning, the confidence score, which rule held it, and a link back to the message. It does not hold the body of your mail. It exists so that any decision can be explained after the fact rather than taken on trust.

How long is anything kept?

WhatHeld for

The decision log is kept because it is the audit trail — deleting it would remove your ability to ask why something was handled the way it was. Whether that record should expire is your call rather than mine, so I have not picked a number. Say a figure and it becomes a setting.

How the morning summary arrives without send permission

It is not sent. It is written directly into your inbox.

You spotted the apparent contradiction in the earlier document, and you were right to. The answer has since become simpler than it was: the summary is placed into your mailbox using the same permission that writes your drafts. Nothing is transmitted, no mail server is involved, and the assistant does not call any send operation to produce it.

One useful consequence — because the summary is written into your mailbox specifically, it cannot be misdirected to anyone else. There is no recipient address that could be mistyped.

Has the send-permission safety test passed?

No — and this is the one open item

The test deliberately attempts to send a message and requires Microsoft to refuse. Microsoft accepts it. The permission to send mail as you is still granted on the underlying Microsoft application, so the test fails.

What this does and does not mean:

  • No part of the assistant sends mail. There is no send step anywhere in it. Every reply it writes sits in your Drafts folder until you press Send.
  • But the permission exists, and I would rather it did not. A promise that rests on the code being correct is weaker than one that rests on the permission being absent.

Removing it needs a Microsoft 365 administrator on partnersperform.com — it is not something I can do from inside the tool. It is bundled into the same access request as the SharePoint work so it is one conversation with them rather than two. I will re-run the test the moment it is done and send you the result either way.
