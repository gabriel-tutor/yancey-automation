Why n8n and not just ChatGPT

The short answer

Most of what I would have claimed as ours, ChatGPT can also do — but only on the right plan.

On the call the answer was “n8n runs 24 hours.” You said you don't need 24 hours, and you were right — that answer was too small. When I went and read OpenAI's documentation properly, I also found that three things I was ready to claim as advantages are not advantages at all. ChatGPT can be held to read-only. It does have an audit trail. Its connectors respect the permissions the person already has.

What survives is narrower and more specific: those controls are workspace admin settings on a paid business plan, and someone has to set them and keep them set. Ours are properties of code that was written not to be able to do the thing, and that is checked against what is actually deployed. That is a real difference, but it is a difference of kind, not of capability.

And it matters that your friend is almost certainly on a personal plan, where none of those controls exist.

The hinge is which plan you are on

This turned out to be the single most useful thing I found. The controls that would make a ChatGPT setup safe for client correspondence are not features of ChatGPT — they are features of a Business, Enterprise or Edu workspace.

Control Personal (Free / Plus) Business Enterprise / Edu
Admin “Action control” — restrict an app to read-only actions Not available Yes Yes
Control over which new actions get enabled later Not available Yes Yes
Compliance Platform — conversation, audit and app logs for eDiscovery / DLP / SIEM Not available Not available Yes, 30-day retention
Scheduled tasks Free: once a day. Paid: up to once an hour Up to once an hour Up to once an hour

So the honest version of “can't we just do it in ChatGPT” is: yes, if you move to a Business workspace at minimum, and to Enterprise if you want the record. On the setup your friend described, there is nothing standing between the model and your mailbox but the model.

Can it send an email by mistake?
n8n, on today's evidence
The build

No workflow contains a send step. It creates a draft and stops. That is checkable rather than promised: every node running on the server is compared line by line against this repository before it goes live.

Honest caveat. The Microsoft app registration still holds Mail.Send. Nothing reaches it, but that means today's guarantee is “no code path can send,” not “Microsoft would refuse.” Removing that permission is an outstanding ask with your admin.

Six weeks from now, can you show why it did that?
both — differently
The build

Every message it looked at is written to a decision log — sender, subject, what it decided, why, and a link back to the mail. Kept indefinitely. Raw message bodies age off the server after seven days; the reasoning does not.

ChatGPT Enterprise / Edu

This is where I was wrong. There is a Compliance Platform giving access to conversation, audit, authentication and app logs, connectable to your eDiscovery, DLP or SIEM tools.

Two real limits. It is Enterprise and Edu only — not Business, not Plus. And it retains 30 days; longer means building something that continuously downloads and stores the logs yourself.

Source “The Compliance Logs Platform retains data for 30 days. If longer retention is desired then consumers should implement a system to continuously download all logs and retain them according to their policies.”
Does it keep running when nobody is watching?
n8n, and for a documented reason
The build

Checks the inbox every minute. Writes your digest into the mailbox at 07:30 Central on weekdays. When something fails it logs it, alerts, and tags the message AI-Error in Outlook, so the failure shows up where you already look.

ChatGPT scheduled tasks

Real, and documented: paid plans support recurring tasks up to once an hour. Free is once a day. Event-triggered tasks can run up to 30 times an hour and 720 a day.

Two things matter for your use. A task cannot run more often than hourly, so minute-by-minute triage is out. And — the one that would actually bite — unattended tasks automatically pause after a period of inactivity.

Source “Tasks cannot run more than once per hour, with unattended tasks automatically pausing after a period of inactivity.” Active task limits vary by tier and are not published per tier.
What actually leaves your tenant?
n8n
The build

Word, PowerPoint and Excel attachments are opened on your own server and only the extracted text is sent onward. PDFs and images go whole, because a scan has no text layer to extract. Clearing one setting turns attachment reading off entirely.

ChatGPT apps

Content the app reads goes to OpenAI — that is what the app is. What you do get: each user authorises their own account, and ChatGPT “only accesses content within that user's existing permissions,” with data encrypted in transit and at rest.

So the boundary is who can see what, rather than what leaves the building. Both are worth having; they are not the same control.

Source Apps respect existing content permissions, so ChatGPT only sees what each person is allowed to access.

Can you change it yourself?
ChatGPT
The build

The switches on your page, yes. Changing a rule is a code change, a build and a verified deployment. That discipline is what makes the guarantees worth anything, and it is also friction, and it runs through me.

ChatGPT

Could it have organised the documents?
n8n, decisively
The build

It opens every .pbix as an archive and reads the Power Query text inside to find which workbooks that report actually reads — then refuses to rename those, and quotes the query as the reason. Names are assembled from parts, so a malformed one cannot be produced. It never overwrites, refuses two files that would collide, caps how much changes in one run, and records every rename: all 27 documents it touched trace back to the name they arrived under.

ChatGPT SharePoint app

It can search and reference SharePoint and OneDrive for work or school content the account can already reach, and — depending on what your organisation approved and your admin enabled — may support creating folders, uploading or updating files, managing sharing links, and working with lists and pages.

None of that includes knowing which workbook a published report reads by path. That failure is silent, and you find out when a dashboard is empty in front of a client.

Not verified Renaming and moving files specifically are not named in the documented action list I could read. Whether they exist as actions is worth checking before assuming either way.

Where ChatGPT is genuinely better

Not a token gesture — the first two we cannot match at any price.

01

It notices things

The suggestion loop your friend described. There is no version of our build that produces it.

02

You are not waiting on anyone

Every change to the current system goes through me. Count that as a cost rather than letting it stay invisible.

03

One place, one subscription

No server, no credentials to rotate, no deployment. Your words: “it doesn't have a separate thing.”

04

The controls are real when you pay for them

Read-only enforcement and a compliance log are proper, documented features — not something I can dismiss. They just are not on a personal plan.

Recommendation

Split it on one line: does the mistake outlive the conversation?

Anything that acts as you and cannot be taken back — sending mail, renaming a client's documents — stays where the constraint is in the code and gets checked against what is deployed. Anything that only advises you belongs in ChatGPT, where it is better than what we built and always will be.

The two are not competitors. They are two answers to “how bad is it if this is wrong.”

Keep in n8n
The acting

Classifying, drafting, the digest, the document organiser. Everything with a consequence that outlives the chat.

Add ChatGPT for
The thinking

Connect it read-only and let it do the thing it is good at: notice patterns, suggest what to automate next, summarise a week.

You asked to try the ChatGPT version and see how it runs. That is the right instinct and I am not arguing against it — but run it beside the current one for two weeks rather than instead of it, and judge it on these four.

  1. Did anything get sent that you had not read first?
  2. Did anything addressed to Chuck get a drafted reply?
  3. Did a scheduled run quietly stop, and how long before you noticed?
  4. Did it suggest something useful our build would never have surfaced?

If four is a yes and one to three are clean, the case for moving is real and I will help you move it. If four is a yes and any of one to three is not, that is your answer: run both.
